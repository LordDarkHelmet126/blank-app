# World data sources

Northern Front’s atlas is original. Commodity picks are for **1985–89**, not the present day. Each region module under `data/world/regions/` names its cities, links, and yields. `tests/world-validate.mjs` checks the structure.

## Shared resource list

`data/world/resources.js` is the only legal yield vocabulary (corn, coal, oil, and the rest). A territory that names something else fails the validator.

## Leadership

Command names in the region files are **fictional**. No real officeholders.

United States ranks follow 1985–89 practice: President, Secretary of Defense, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, fleet and theater commanders, and field grades. The occupation prefecture and the resistance net are fictional commands on top of that continuity government. Notable officers are posted by region (Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Plains, Mountain West, Southwest, Pacific, Alaska and Hawaii). Canadian ranks follow the unified Canadian Forces of the 1980s: Prime Minister, Minister of National Defence, Chief of the Defence Staff, Forces Mobile Command, Maritime Command, Air Command, and regional commanders. French and English names are used where the posting makes that ordinary. They use the same personality ids as `data/officers.json` and the same WAR / INT / POL / CHR stats, but they are not copied onto the week-0 actor list, so the Cheyenne campaign does not gain a new court.

Region 3 uses the same dormant slots. A country’s roster now scales with its size: one or two cities keep a head of state and a field officer; three to seven cities add a defense minister; eight or more cities keep the full five slots, and only the large countries add extra region commanders. That is why Anguilla is two officers and Mexico is about thirty, not why every island had five.

Ranks follow the 1980s force actually in the field, and no real officeholder is named. Mexico: a president, SEDENA (Secretaría de la Defensa Nacional), and SEMAR (the navy). The file posts fictional commanders at the twelve region seats that SEDENA’s later published map uses (Mexico City, Mexicali, Mazatlán, Monterrey, Guadalajara, Veracruz, Tuxtla Gutiérrez, Ixcotel, Acapulco, Mérida, Torreón, and Irapuato), plus naval zones at Manzanillo, Tampico, Progreso, La Paz, and Ciudad del Carmen. A mid-decade study still counted nine regions and thirty-six zones, one zone per state except the doubles in Oaxaca, Guerrero, Veracruz, and (from 1984) Chiapas. Guatemala, Honduras, and El Salvador: the armed forces and a civilian president. Nicaragua: a president and the EPS (Ejército Popular Sandinista). Costa Rica: Public Security, the Civil Guard, and the Rural Guard, because there is no army. Panama: the Defense Forces, plus a fictional US Army colonel at Balboa for the canal garrison. Cuba: the FAR (Fuerzas Armadas Revolucionarias), with western, central, and eastern commands. Haiti: French and Creole names in the armed forces. The Dominican Republic: the armed forces. Jamaica: the JDF. Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands: a governor and the National Guard. The Bahamas: the RBDF. British dependencies use a chief minister and, on the one-island posts, a single police or defence field officer. Guadeloupe and Martinique use a prefect and the gendarmerie. Grenada’s staff is a prime minister, national security, and a police inspector, the post-1983 arrangement. Trinidad and Tobago: the TTDF. Spanish, English, French, and Creole names follow the posting.

South American officers are fictional and dormant. Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Peru, and Bolivia use a civilian president over a general staff (Bolivia’s palace is civilian again by the middle of the decade). Brazil’s president is civilian from 1985, with an army minister who still thinks in the pre-1988 map. Argentina and Uruguay are civilian from 1983 and 1985. Chile’s head of state is a fictional captain general; the 1988 plebiscite is noted and no real junta member is named. Paraguay’s president is a fictional general until the February 1989 change. Suriname’s head of state is a fictional lieutenant colonel for a decade of military government. Guyana is civilian. French Guiana is a prefect and the gendarmerie. The Falklands are a British governor and a Royal Marines major. Names are Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, English, or French to match the posting.

## Later regions

The same resource list and validator apply. Eastern Europe and the USSR are next. Country notes will still come from the public yearbooks above plus the CIA *World Factbook* editions of 1987, 1988, and 1989. No road on this sheet crosses into that theatre.
